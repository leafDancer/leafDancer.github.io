01 / The method

Sidestep the Master equation.

Keep the economy’s heterogeneity. Change the state representation used to make decisions.

In heterogeneous-agent models with aggregate risk, equilibrium prices generally depend on the full cross-sectional distribution. Tracking that distribution as a state variable creates a high-dimensional Master equation.

SRL works with a low-dimensional policy π(s, z, p) and evaluates lifetime utility along simulated equilibrium price paths. It combines reinforcement learning with structural knowledge of individual dynamics.

  1. 01

    Use the structure you know.

    Budget constraints and idiosyncratic transitions are encoded in the policy-dependent transition matrix Aπ. Differentiate the simulated objective through these known individual dynamics.

  2. 02

    Sample equilibrium price paths.

    Simulate aggregate shocks and prices to estimate expected lifetime utility. Policies double as demand schedules, so markets can clear along each rollout, including under a candidate policy.

  3. 03

    Improve the policy, taking prices as given.

    Structural policy gradient updates the low-dimensional policy. A stop-gradient through price formation implements competitive price-taking; the individual does not internalize its effect on equilibrium prices.

With the restricted decision state, the equilibrium concept is a sequential restricted perceptions equilibrium. The full distribution continues to evolve in the environment. Policies can also be extended to condition on price histories.

Inside the structural policy gradient

Known individual transitions carry the gradient; simulated aggregate histories supply the price paths. Monte Carlo sampling still introduces uncertainty in the estimated objective.

04 / Global and local solutions

Where nonlinearities matter.

The portfolio example illustrates differences between SRL and first-, second-, and third-order sequence-space approximations.

A global comparison beyond small shocks.

In the two-asset model, local approximations can deviate materially from the SRL solution as the aggregate shock moves away from the steady state. Higher approximation order does not uniformly close the gap in this example.

05 / Further validation

What changes the learned policy?

Inspect how a richer price history affects consumption policies.

Does adding a lagged price change decisions?

Richer price information.

In the Huggett experiment, allowing the policy to condition on one lagged bond price changes consumption policies only slightly over the displayed states.

This is an empirical robustness check in the benchmark, rather than a general result that current prices are sufficient in every economy.
